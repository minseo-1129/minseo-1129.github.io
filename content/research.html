Research

I study how intelligent systems can participate in care—from structuring individual interactions, to supporting reflection and self-regulation, to designing and evaluating the wider conditions under which AI becomes part of human care.

F-01 Designing ForMe’s Task-Based Dialogue Flow Goal–Plan–Do–Check → failure-tolerant dialogue architecture. F-02 From a Child–Agent Interaction to a School–Family Support System Scaling a child-facing intervention into a multi-stakeholder support ecosystem.
REDI · Designing reflection with personal data How can everyday behaviour become something a person can understand and act on?
D-01 Designing from How Adolescents Already Record Their Lives Natural self-recording behaviour → design requirements. D-02 What Should a Personalised Health Report Actually Personalise? Domain, timescale, reference and interpretation before language generation. D-03 Turning Self-Tracking Habits into a Digital Phenotype Collection System Low-friction recording, feedback and routine-aware EMA. D-04 From Self-Recording to Self-Regulation Uncertainty, personal experimentation and the next decision.
Multimodal Digital Phenotyping What complementary signals can sit alongside self-report?
M-01 Exploring Multimodal Signals for Children’s Mental Health Screening Heart-rate variability, gaze and voice as exploratory complementary evidence.
RECO · Designing AI inside human care What role should generative AI occupy when care already involves human relationships?
R-01 Defining the Role of AI Before Designing the Agent Participatory design → bounded intermediary for sense-making. R-02 Translating Stakeholder Expectations into Agent Architecture Stakeholder expectations → agents, tools, policies and infrastructure. R-03 When Agent Architecture Becomes Interaction Design Single-prompt vs multi-node organisation as an interaction-design variable. R-04 Designing Summaries as Part of the Interaction Memory, data agency and mediated visibility into human care.
