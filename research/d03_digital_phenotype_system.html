D–03

REDI

Turning Self-Tracking Habits into a Digital Phenotype Collection System

REDI combined planner-based recording, low-friction tags, visual feedback, and routine-aware EMA to make active phenotype collection useful enough to sustain.

Period
Jul 2023 – Oct 2023
Type
Proof of Concept · Digital Phenotyping
Participants
34 formative · 16 proof-of-concept
Research question

How can active digital phenotype collection be made useful enough to sustain in everyday adolescent life?

01 / mechanisms

From observations to interaction mechanisms

The formative diary study revealed what adolescents already valued about recording. REDI translated those observations into a collection system organised around four interaction mechanisms.

Familiarity Planner-like recording

Daily behaviour appeared in a timetable structure adolescents already understood.

Low friction Tag shortcuts

Frequently recorded activities could be selected rather than typed repeatedly.

Interpretability Visual feedback

Records were made visible as patterns and interpretive feedback rather than functioning only as research inputs.

Routine sensitivity Supportive EMA

Mood prompts were scheduled around school and after-school life and written to feel supportive.

02 / prototype

Simulating the experience before engineering the full system

Before implementing the complete application, we tested whether these mechanisms worked together in everyday life.

Notion Simulated planner + tag recording
KakaoTalk Simulated scheduled EMA prompts
Researcher reports Simulated visual feedback

These tools were not presented as the REDI product itself. They functioned as a service prototype for testing the intended interaction model before committing to full engineering.

03 / engagement

Engagement became evidence about the interaction design

Sixteen adolescents used the service prototype for two weeks. All participants maintained 24-hour activity records across the study period, although some entries were completed retrospectively.

16 proof-of-concept participants
2 weeks daily activity + mood recording
72% average EMA response rate after excluding one misunderstanding
EMA participation pattern
Individual rate
35–92% individual range · 72% average
Daily average
62–79% across the 14 days · 72% average

Visible gaps in the planner sometimes helped participants notice incomplete records and fill them in. The representation itself therefore became part of the engagement mechanism.

04 / system

The mechanisms reinforced one another

The useful outcome was not attributable to one isolated feature. Several choices worked together to make repeated self-recording manageable.

Familiar Recognisable planner structure.
Low effort Fast tags and short EMA.
Visible Missing data could be noticed and completed.
Supportive Prompts fit real routines rather than arbitrary research intervals.
Useful Feedback returned meaning from the records.

The collection system was therefore designed as an interaction ecology: input, prompting, visibility, and feedback each affected whether the other parts remained worth using.

Design takeaway

Sustained self-recording was supported by mutually reinforcing interaction choices—not by a single engagement feature.
