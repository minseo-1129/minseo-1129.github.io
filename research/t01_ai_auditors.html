T–01

AI Evaluation / Red Teaming

Calibrating AI Auditors Before Trusting Their Judgements

If LLMs are used to evaluate other AI systems, the evaluator itself first becomes a measurement-design problem.

Period
Feb 2026 – Aug 2026
Type
AI Evaluation · LLM-as-Judge · Self-Determination Theory
Evaluation set
30 human participants · 30 synthetic users
Research question

How can synthetic users and AI auditors be calibrated against human participants before we rely on them for pre-deployment evaluation?

01 / risk

Before giving AI to young people

A psychologically sensitive conversational system creates an uncomfortable evaluation problem: discovering a failure through repeated exposure can itself place vulnerable participants at risk.

Technical testing is necessary, but it cannot tell us whether an interaction makes someone feel controlled, incapable, misunderstood, dismissed, or pushed toward action before they are ready.

Synthetic userEncounter experimental agent variants
AI auditorEstimate psychological need support / frustration
Human benchmarkCalibrate whether synthetic judgement is close enough to trust

The aim was not to replace human evaluation. Thirty human participants remained the benchmark, with 30 synthetic AI users used to test how closely the synthetic evaluation could be calibrated to human judgements.

02 / persona

Encode causes, not conclusions

A synthetic persona can accidentally contain the answer to the outcome we later ask it to evaluate. If a profile explicitly says that a person dislikes being told what to do, then asking whether an agent supported their autonomy risks prompting the result rather than simulating it.

Avoid

Persona statements that directly encode the psychological judgement being measured.

Prefer

Upstream characteristics—everyday context, symptoms, emotion regulation, dispositions, self-efficacy, and stress—that may influence the experience.

A synthetic user should contain conditions that may shape an outcome. It should not contain the outcome we are trying to predict.

03 / calibration

The evaluator is part of the measurement design

The observed judgement changed when we changed how the evaluation was framed.

Measurement levelBehaviour → task

Task-level judgement reduced aggregate error relative to judging broader behaviour-level constructs.

PerspectiveThird person → first person

Asking the synthetic participant to report its own experience brought estimates closer to the human benchmark.

ContextThin → situated

Context improved first-person evaluation across autonomy, competence, and relatedness dimensions.

EvaluatorGenerator → stronger reasoner

A stronger evaluation model moved closer to the human benchmark than simply reusing the conversational model.

The implication is methodological: an AI auditor is not a neutral ruler. Perspective, abstraction, context, and model capability all affect what it measures.

04 / instrument

Synthetic user as an experimental instrument

The iterations changed the role of the synthetic user. It is less useful to think of it as a fixed artificial person and more useful to think of the whole configuration as a measurement instrument.

Synthetic response = Person × Context × Measurement × Model

Who the persona is matters, but so does the situation, the question, the abstraction level, the perspective from which it is answered, and the model that performs the reasoning.

That is why calibration matters: changing any one part can change the synthetic “participant” we think we are observing.

06 / boundary

A mismatch can define the boundary of trust

The remaining error around relatedness is not simply a failed metric. It identifies where synthetic evaluation may not yet be trustworthy enough.

Autonomy and competence leave substantial evidence in the observable interaction: options offered, control over direction, organisation of a problem, or clarity about a next step. Relatedness can depend more strongly on whether somebody felt cared for, connected, or personally understood.

Use synthetic evaluation for

Repeated pre-deployment testing, surfacing likely failure modes, and comparing experimental configurations.

Keep humans essential for

Constructs and experiences that synthetic evaluation cannot reproduce with acceptable calibration.

The goal is therefore not “Can AI replace participants?” It is “What psychological failures can we surface before a vulnerable participant encounters the system?”

Design takeaway

Synthetic evaluation should not be trusted because it looks human-like. It should be trusted only to the extent that it has been calibrated against humans.
