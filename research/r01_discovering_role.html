R–01

RECO / Reflective Companion

Defining the Role of AI Before Designing the Agent

Participatory design with young people and link workers shifted the opportunity from automating social prescribing to supporting sense-making between human encounters.

Period
May 2025 – Jul 2025
Type
Participatory Design · Youth Mental Health · Social Prescribing
Participants
8 young people aged 16–25 · 3 link workers
Research question

Where does support currently break down, and what role would young people and practitioners actually want technology to occupy?

01 / framing

Before asking what the agent should do

Conversational AI can recommend, coach, remind, assess, check in, and provide information. But selecting a role because the technology can perform it risks solving the wrong problem—or interfering with relationships that already matter.

For youth social prescribing, the design process began with where support currently breaks down and what role young people and practitioners would actually want technology to occupy.

02 / participation

Designing with both sides of the care relationship

Separate participatory-design workshops involved eight young people and three link workers. Participants mapped social-prescribing journeys, identified barriers, and imagined possible forms of conversational support.

03 / expectations

Stakeholders imagined different but connected roles

Rather than choosing one impressive feature, we treated the role lists as evidence about what each stakeholder hoped the system might make easier.

The lists overlap in places, but their motivations differ: young people emphasised continuity and low-pressure support, while link workers also needed visibility, records, and practical service coordination.

04 / role

Sense-making between formal sessions became the central opportunity

A recurring need connected several stakeholder expectations: young people often needed to articulate what happened between formal care encounters.

Link-worker sessionGoals and activities are co-planned.
Prescription participationEmotional and practical challenges unfold outside formal care.
Everyday check-inReflect on what happened and what it meant.
Next sessionBe prepared to share what has been useful.
05 / boundary

The Reflective Companion became a bounded intermediary

The concept was positioned between everyday participation and formal human support—not as another autonomous mental-health service.

Everyday experienceActivities, emotions, barriers, goals.
Reflective CompanionLow-pressure space for articulation and sense-making.
Human careMore informed follow-up when the young person chooses to carry something forward.
Notan autonomous activity prescriber
Nota source of clinical judgement
Nota replacement for the link worker
Nota virtual friend

The role was deliberately bounded so that AI could support reflection without claiming the authority or relationship of human care.

Design takeaway

Define the role before the feature: the agent became a bounded intermediary for sense-making between human encounters.
