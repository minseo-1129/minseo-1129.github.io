M–01

Multimodal Digital Phenotyping

Exploring Multimodal Signals for Children’s Mental Health Screening

A tablet-based assessment combined heart-rate variability, gaze, and voice to explore signals beyond self-report.

Period
2024
Type
Digital Biomarkers · Child Mental Health · Multimodal Assessment
Participants
54 children aged 7–11
Research question

Can multimodal behavioural and physiological signals provide complementary evidence for children’s mental-health screening beyond self-report?

01 / problem

When self-report is not enough

Mental-health screening often relies heavily on self-report or caregiver report. For younger children, both sources can be incomplete.

Children may have difficulty recognising or verbalising internal experiences, while adults can only infer those experiences from what they observe. We explored whether behavioural and physiological signals collected through a familiar device could provide complementary evidence.

Self-report

Direct access to the child’s account, but dependent on recognition, language, and willingness to report.

Observed / sensed data

Potentially complementary evidence, but still requiring careful interpretation and validation.

02 / collection

Three modalities through a tablet

The assessment combined three forms of digital biomarker collection rather than searching for one universal marker.

PhysiologyHeart-rate variability

The tablet camera supported remote photoplethysmography for estimating HRV-related features.

AttentionEye tracking

The front-facing camera measured gaze during tasks using emotional facial stimuli.

ExpressionVoice

Free-speech and picture-description tasks were used to extract acoustic characteristics.

The design question was whether the modalities could complement one another—especially when any single source might provide only a partial view.

03 / signals

Different outcomes were associated with different signals

The strongest relationships were not distributed evenly across modalities.

Depression HRV + gaze

Depressive symptoms were associated with physiological measures and time spent attending to sad facial expressions.

Anxiety Mostly HRV

Anxiety was most consistently explained by heart-rate-variability features rather than gaze or voice.

Stress Physiological pattern

Stress was associated with HRV-related features, with some relationships changing across younger and older grade groups.

Voice features did not show significant overall correlations with the clinical scales in the full sample. The broader pattern therefore suggests complementarity rather than equal contribution from every modality.

04 / models

Selected signal combinations explained variance in symptom scores

Regression models provided a second view of the same pattern: the amount of variance explained differed substantially by outcome.

Anxiety produced the strongest explanatory model in this exploratory analysis, followed by depression and stress. These are not diagnostic performance scores; they show where combinations of physiological and behavioural features may warrant further validation.

05 / development

Development is part of the measurement problem

Relationships between biomarkers were not necessarily stable across age groups.

Additional analyses separated lower and upper elementary-school grades and found different patterns for some HRV and voice features. That matters because biomarkers established in adults—or even in older children—cannot automatically be assumed to carry the same meaning in younger children.

PhysiologyAutonomic regulation changes with development.
AttentionVisual attention and emotional processing are still developing.
ExpressionSpeech, language, and affective expression vary substantially across childhood.

The study involved 54 children aged 7–11 and did not recruit clinically diagnosed depression or anxiety groups. The authors explicitly frame the work as exploratory and not suitable, by itself, for clinical diagnosis.

Design takeaway

Multimodal signals can complement self-report—but combining more signals does not make a system diagnostic.
