D–02

REDI

What Should a Personalised Health Report Actually Personalise?

Adolescents wanted different kinds of feedback for sleep, study, exercise, and leisure—suggesting that personalisation begins before the message is generated.

Period
Mar 2023 – Jun 2023
Type
Intelligent Visualisation · Card Sorting
Participants
14 Korean adolescents aged 14–18
Research question

What exactly should the system personalise when it turns lifestyle data into visualisation and feedback?

01 / framing

Personalisation is more than changing the wording

When personal data become feedback, the first personalisation decision may happen before any message is generated.

Sleep happens every day. Exercise may happen only several times a week. Study may be judged against a personally meaningful goal. For leisure or media use, an adolescent may not know what an appropriate reference point should be.

Common framing

Personalise the wording of one report.

Emerging framing

Decide what kind of report makes sense for this behaviour before writing it.

02 / study

Building reports from participants’ own data

Fourteen Korean adolescents aged 14–18 recorded four lifestyle domains for two weeks. We then produced different report structures from their own records and used card sorting and interviews to compare what was useful.

Domain Sleep

Highly repetitive and naturally suited to daily summaries.

Domain Study

Often evaluated against personal goals.

Domain Exercise

Occurs less frequently and is better understood over a longer window.

Domain Leisure

May benefit from an external reference when personal standards are unclear.

Participants compared decisions about summary level, comparison window, evaluation reference, and how much interpretation the report should add.

03 / representation

The useful representation changed by behaviour

A daily sleep pattern and occasional exercise activity should not necessarily be summarised, compared, or evaluated using the same information structure.

Decision Examples participants compared
Summary level Daily vs weekly
Comparison Recent three-day trend · week-to-week · weekday vs weekend
Evaluation Personal target vs external recommendation
Interpretation Additional patterns that help explain what the data may mean

Participants often preferred their own goals for study and exercise, while objective guidance could be more useful for some leisure behaviours where an appropriate amount was less obvious.

04 / meaning

Interpretation mattered more than another graph

Participants were especially interested when feedback revealed a pattern that was difficult to see directly in raw records.

Base layer Recorded data

Sleep, study, exercise, leisure, and their timing.

Display Summaries · trends · comparisons

Make behaviour visible across meaningful time windows and reference points.

Interpretation “What does this pattern mean for me?”

Use relationships and context to turn records into something a person can understand.

Interpretation is built on display rather than positioned as an equal alternative to it.

05 / decision

Personalisation as a sequence of decisions

The findings suggested a dynamic reporting system could reason through a series of decisions before generating the final feedback.

Decision Question
Domain What behaviour is this?
Frequency How often does it naturally occur?
Timescale What window is meaningful?
Reference Personal target or external recommendation?
Interpretation What pattern would actually help?

Only after those choices does language generation become the final layer of personalisation.

Design takeaway

Personalisation should not stop at representing a person through data and numbers; meaningful behaviour change requires understanding the values that give those data meaning.
