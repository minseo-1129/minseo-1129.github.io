R–02

RECO / Reflective Companion

Translating Stakeholder Expectations into Agent Architecture

Mapping expectations across the social-prescribing journey made visible how qualitative needs became agents, tools, memory, infrastructure, and interaction policies.

Period
Aug 2025 – Sep 2025
Type
Participatory Design · Agent Architecture · Design Traceability
Participants
8 young people · 3 link workers
Research question

How can stakeholder expectations be translated into an LLM-agent architecture without losing sight of where those decisions came from?

01 / translation

Expectations are not yet a system

Participatory design surfaced roles stakeholders imagined for AI. Implementation still required another layer of interpretation.

“Personalisation” could become persistent memory, a user profile, adaptive prompting, or a dedicated component. “Companionship” could become its own agent—or a cross-cutting interaction policy. Each choice introduces different assumptions about agency, privacy, memory, and responsibility.

The design problem therefore became one of traceability: keeping visible how a stakeholder expectation became an architectural decision.

02 / journey

Mapping expectations across the social-prescribing journey

Before deciding what should become an agent, tool, database, or interaction rule, expectations were mapped back onto where they appeared in the care journey.

Formal sessionGoals, context, and support are discussed.
Between sessionsDaily changes and emerging needs accumulate.
Community activityParticipation creates new experience and constraints.
Reflection / adaptationExperience is interpreted and future participation reconsidered.
Next sessionRelevant insight can return to human follow-up.

This helped separate what needed to happen inside the conversation from what belonged to the wider service infrastructure.

03 / axis

The mapping revealed a central conversational axis

Several features depended on information that could only emerge gradually through dialogue: goals, preferences, barriers, mood, activity experience, and changing interests.

Isolated featureArchitectural interpretation
Check-in MateOne possible role among activity recommendation, goal planning, profiling, and companionship.Primary reflective loopA central conversation through which information needed by other functions can emerge over time.
Dependent functionGoal support
Planning and tracking depend on goals, progress, and barriers that emerge through ongoing check-ins.
Dependent functionActivity connection
Recommendation depends on preferences, contextual constraints, and previous activity experience.
Background toolContextual profile
Persistent attributes can support personalisation without independently driving the conversation.
Interaction-wideSocial companion
Empathy, support, and non-judgement operate as a policy across components rather than as one separate agent.
Wider service / infrastructureSession logging + resource support
These functions strengthen continuity and professional workflow without defining the central young-person dialogue.

Check-in therefore became less like another module and more like the conversational axis around which the rest of the system could organise.

04 / consolidation

Similar expectations did not need separate components

The ten stakeholder themes did not become ten technical components. Expectations that shared operations or information could be consolidated before architecture was assigned.

Stakeholder expectationsConsolidated mechanism
Goal Planner + Goal TrackerGoal Manager
Personal Profiler + Interest BankContextual Profiler
Activity Connector + Activity RecommenderActivity Connector

The architecture therefore moved away from reproducing workshop labels one-to-one and toward organising the system around reusable operations and information structures.

05 / architecture

Not every expectation became an agent

A useful question was not only “Should this exist?” but “What kind of architectural role should it have?”

ExpectationArchitectural roleReason
Check-in MatePrimary AgentSustained open-ended reflection.
Goal ManagerSub-agentGoal-related topics trigger planning and adjustment.
Activity ConnectorSub-agentSuggest activity options when relevant needs come up.
Contextual ProfilerToolExtract and save personal attributes.
Social CompanionInteraction PolicyEmpathy and non-judgement should shape every component.
Session LoggerInfrastructureContinuity and administration without its own dialogue.
Design takeaway

Stakeholders may be responding to the same underlying challenge, but what they expect from AI changes subtly with their role and perspective.
